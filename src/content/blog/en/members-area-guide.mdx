**Astro Rocket 2.4.0 has a members area.**

You can keep part of your site private — a post, a page, a whole series of
them — readable only by the people you choose.

It works like this. You register your members yourself: you collect their email
addresses and add them to a list in your project. Those people can then sign in
with that email address. They ask for a link, it arrives in their inbox, they
click it, and they are in. No password.

Anyone whose email address is not on your list cannot get in.

Nothing else about your site changes. No database, no membership platform, no
different hosting.

## Try it on this site

This site is running it, so you can see the whole thing without setting
anything up. It takes about ten seconds and asks for no email address:

1. Open [A Members-Only Post](/blog/members-only-example). You will see the
   title, the date and the description, and a lock where the article should be.
2. Click **Sign in**.
3. On the sign-in page, click **Sign in as a demo member**.
4. Go back to the post. The article is there.

Sign out again from `/members` and it locks.

## What a visitor sees

A private post keeps its place on your blog. The card shows the title, the date
and the description, with a lock on it, so people can see that the post exists
and what it is about.

Opening it gives them those same words and a way to sign in. Not a 404, and not
a page that looks broken.

The article itself is not in that page. It is not hidden with CSS, not blurred,
and not waiting for JavaScript to reveal it — it is never sent. View the source
of a locked post and the text is not there.

Private posts are also kept out of your RSS feed and out of the site search.

## What a member sees

They meet this page. It uses your own colours and follows the reader's light or

They type the email address you registered them with. A link arrives, valid for
fifteen minutes. They click it and they are signed in for thirty days on that
device.

From then on, private posts read exactly like any other post on your site.

They also get a page at `/members` listing everything their membership covers,
and `/members/account` showing their email address and when their sign-in runs
out.

## Registering your members

Your members are a list in `src/config/members.config.ts`:

```ts
members: [
  { email: 'anna@example.com', name: 'Anna' },
  { email: 'ben@example.com', name: 'Ben' },
],
```

Adding someone is one line and a deploy. Removing someone is deleting that
line. Because the list is part of your project, the record of who had access,
and when, is your version history — there is no admin screen to build and none
to secure.

A sign-in lasts thirty days, and you set how long: `sessionDays` in the same
file. Someone you remove cannot sign in again, and a shorter setting is what
you want if you would rather an existing sign-in stopped sooner.

How people get onto that list is up to you and happens away from the site: they
bought the course, signed the contract, joined the club, paid the invoice.

**Someone not on the list gets nothing.** They can type their email address and
press the button, but no link is ever sent. They see the same *check your email*
page a real member sees, because a page that said "no such member" could be used
to work out who your members are.

That is worth planning for: give people a way to ask you for access. A line
under the sign-in form, or a sentence in a locked post's description, saves
somebody waiting for an email that is not coming.

## What makes it secure

**There is no password anywhere, so there is no password to steal.** Most
serious breaches are stolen password databases. This has none — not hashed,
not encrypted, none. There is also no member database: your list is an email
address and a name, in your own project, on your own host. No third party ever
sees who your members are.

What a member carries is a cookie, and it is protected on several fronts:

- **It is signed with HMAC-SHA256.** Change one character of it and the server
  rejects it. Nobody can edit their own cookie to grant themselves a tier.
- **Signatures are compared in constant time**, so the comparison cannot be
  used to guess a valid one character by character.
- **The cookie is `httpOnly`**, so no JavaScript on the page can read it — a
  script injected into your site cannot steal a member's session.
- **It is `secure` and `sameSite`**, so it only travels over HTTPS and is not
  sent from other websites.

And around that:

- **The article is never sent to someone who may not read it.** Not hidden,
  not blurred — absent from the response.
- **Member pages are never cached.** They are sent as `private, no-store`, so
  no CDN or proxy can keep one member's page and hand it to somebody else.
- **The sign-in form cannot be used to discover your members.** It answers the
  same way for every email address.
- **Sign-in requests are rate limited** — five a minute per email address — so
  nobody can flood an inbox with links.
- **The build refuses to run** if the members area is on without a signing
  secret. It is not possible to deploy this with the lock accidentally open.
- **Sign-in links expire in fifteen minutes**, and a link only ever works for
  the email address it was issued to.

## Turning it on

**Two environment variables:**

```bash
MEMBERS_SESSION_SECRET=   # openssl rand -base64 32
RESEND_API_KEY=           # sends the sign-in links
RESEND_FROM_EMAIL=        # the email address links are sent from
```

**Enable it** in `src/config/members.config.ts`:

```ts
enabled: true,
```

**Mark a post private** by adding one line to its frontmatter:

```yaml
access: members
```

That post now needs a signed-in member. Its URL does not change, and it stays
on your blog.

### Trying it without an email service

Before you sign up for anything: in development the sign-in link is printed to
your terminal instead of being emailed.

```bash
pnpm dev
```

Open `/members/login`, type an email address from your list, and the link
appears in your console. Click it and you are signed in. The whole feature
works on your own machine with no account anywhere.

## More than one level

If everybody sees the same thing, you can ignore this section — `access:
members` covers any member, and the word "tier" never appears in your project.

If you need levels, name them:

```yaml
access: pro
```

and give the member the tier:

```ts
{ email: 'ben@example.com', name: 'Ben', tiers: ['pro'] },
```

Ben reads it; a member without that tier sees the locked page instead.

## Nothing changes until you turn it on

The theme ships with the members area switched off, and off means no member
pages are built at all. An existing site that upgrades and changes nothing
produces exactly the output it did before.

Two rules follow from that. A post marked `access: members` while the feature
is off is hidden rather than published. And enabling it without a session
secret stops the build, with a message telling you how to make one — a lock
that quietly fails open would be worse than no lock.

## If you need a client portal

The members area gives people access to your content. A client portal is a
different thing: everyone signs in and sees **their own records** — their
projects, their invoices, their documents, their progress through a course.

Those records are different for every business, which is why they are not part
of a theme. They are built for one site, on top of this one, and that is work I
do.

I run one myself. The client portal on
[hansmartens.dev](https://hansmartens.dev) shows each of my clients their own
projects and invoices, behind the same kind of sign-in described here.

Built on top of the members area, that can include:

- **Per-customer records** — projects, invoices, documents, downloads, order
  history.
- **Payments through Stripe** — paid memberships, subscriptions, one-off
  purchases, and people signing themselves up by paying.
- **A course area** with lessons in order and progress kept per student.
- **Anything that has to know who is looking**, rather than only whether they
  are allowed in.

The theme stays free and stays yours. You pay for the part that is built for
you, and you keep it.
